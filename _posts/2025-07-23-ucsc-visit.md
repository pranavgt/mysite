I’ve spent a lot of time on university campuses. Between years of competitive soccer and recruitment visits, I think I have seen my fair share of "pristine". But my visit to UC Santa Cruz for the closing day of the Science Internship Program (SIP) was a genuine surprise and a special treat.

The natural beauty of the campus itself was a hidden surprise. It is basically hidden inside a redwood forest, but there was something else: having spent the last few weeks in the usual Bay Area tech circles and "in-crowd" hack spaces, UCSC felt different. It has this quiet, meditative, but incredibly focused energy of : "I know what I am doing, and I don't need the trendy, in-crowd posturing to advertise it".

The afternoon started with a conversation with Professor Raja Guha Thakurta and ended with a front-row seat to the SIP closing presentations. Seeing high schoolers diving into authentic, open-ended research reminded me of my own "recalibration". That familiar mix of trepidation and excited optimism in the room was very relatable.

As I wrap up my time at Penn, I’m at a bit of a crossroads as well. There’s the obvious path straight into industry, but the "research for good" philosophy I felt across the UCSC campus has me rethinking things. I am strongly considering if strengthening my research at a place that aligns so closely with my values will be the right move toward the equity-focused finance products I want to build.
